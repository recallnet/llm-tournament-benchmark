This study investigates how Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) fundamentally transforms asset return prediction models within Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO) frameworks. Unlike traditional approaches that minimize uniform prediction errors such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), DFL integrates downstream portfolio optimization objectives directly into the training process. Our empirical analysis reveals three key findings: (1) DFL leads to extreme portfolio concentration, consistently utilizing only the minimum number of assets allowed by constraints, contrasting with MSE-trained models that maintain diversification; (2) DFL systematically induces prediction biases aligned with portfolio decisions, where selected assets exhibit positive bias while excluded assets show negative bias; and (3) DFL amplifies directional predictions, with positive return assets becoming increasingly overestimated. Theoretically, we demonstrate that the DFL gradient incorporates inter-asset correlations through an inverse covariance matrix tilting mechanism, fundamentally differing from MSE's independent treatment of prediction errors. These findings resolve the paradox of why DFL achieves superior portfolio performance despite higher prediction errors, revealing that strategic prediction biases are features that enhance decision quality rather than errors to be corrected.
